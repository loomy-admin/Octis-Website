How it's worked out

What 916, 750 and 585 mean

The hallmark number stamped on a piece names its karat: 916 is 22K, 750 is 18K, 585 is 14K and 375 is 9K. The number is how many parts in 1,000 are pure gold, so it also gives the rate: 22K = 24K × 91.6%, 18K × 75%, 14K × 58.5% and 9K × 37.5%. Each rate is rounded to the rupee.

Grams and tola

1 tola = 10 g. So the rate for one tola is the same as the rate for 10 g.

Worked example

An example, step by step

Say 24K gold is ₹1,00,000 per 10 g (a round number, not today's rate).

  1. 1 22K (916) ₹1,00,000 × 91.6% ₹91,600
  2. 2 18K (750) × 75% ₹75,000
  3. 3 14K (585) × 58.5% ₹58,500
  4. 4 9K (375) × 37.5% ₹37,500
  5. 5 One tola of 22K same as 10 g of 22K ₹91,600

And 50 g of jewellery is 5.000 tola.

Questions about purity and weight

It's the hallmark stamp for 22K. 750 is the stamp for 18K, 585 for 14K and 375 for 9K.

Multiply by 91.6% (0.916). At ₹1,00,000 per 10 g for 24K, 22K is ₹91,600.

22 ÷ 24 is 91.67%, but 22K jewellery is hallmarked 916, which is 91.6% pure gold. The market quotes 22K at 91.6% of 24K, so OCTIS does too, and 18K, 14K and 9K follow their hallmarks the same way (75%, 58.5%, 37.5%).

10 g. OCTIS counts a tola as 10 g everywhere, so the rate per tola is the rate per 10 g.
